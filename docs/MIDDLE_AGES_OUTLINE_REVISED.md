# Middle Ages Track: 30-Lesson Outline (Source-Based)
## Based on Church 101 & Schaff's History of the Christian Church

---

## **PART I: FOUNDATIONS & THE THREE PILLARS (Lessons 1–5)**

These lessons establish what medieval Christianity was, its defining characteristics, and the three institutional forms that dominated it (papacy, monasticism, scholasticism).

1. **What Was Medieval Christianity? (A.D. 590–1073)**
   - Definition: transition period between ancient Rome and the modern era
   - Geography: mainly European theater; Islam's conquest of Asia/North Africa
   - Key theme: Christianity + Germanic vigor + Roman law = medieval synthesis
   - Sources: Schaff's introduction; Church 101 overviews

2. **The Three Pillars: Papacy, Monasticism, Scholasticism**
   - How these three forms shaped medieval Christianity
   - Why they dominated institutional church life
   - Their successes and limitations
   - Source: Schaff's thesis; Church 101's treatment of each century

3. **The Nations of Medieval Christianity: Kelts, Teutons, Slavs**
   - Kelt: quick-witted, eloquent, but unstable; later devoted to Rome
   - Teuton: disciplined, orderly, capable of civilization; the "advancing races"
   - Slav: isolated from main current; later Eastern Orthodox dominated
   - Greek: maintained independence; grew apart from West
   - Source: Schaff's detailed ethnographic analysis

4. **Gregory the Great (A.D. 590–604): Founder of Medieval Papacy**
   - The last of the Fathers, first of the Popes
   - Organized missionary movements on a grand scale
   - Built papal administrative infrastructure
   - Crisis: nominal Christianity ("form of godliness without power")
   - Source: Church 101 + Schaff on Gregory's transformative role

5. **St. Benedict and Early Monasticism (6th Century)**
   - Life and withdrawal to monastic life
   - The Rule of St. Benedict: governance, worship, work
   - Monasteries as centers of learning and spiritual power
   - Spread across medieval Europe
   - Source: Church 101 on Benedict; Schaff on monastic development

## **PART II: MISSIONARY MOVEMENTS & NATIONAL CONVERSIONS (Lessons 6–17)**

The heart of medieval Christianity: how entire peoples moved from paganism to faith. Schaff devotes massive attention to this; Church 101 treats it century-by-century.

### **The British Isles & Ireland (Lessons 6–9)**

6. **St. Patrick and the Conversion of Ireland (5th–6th Centuries)**
   - Patrick's capture, escape, training
   - Return to Ireland as missionary bishop
   - Unique Irish Christianity: monastic, independent of Rome initially
   - Irish Church after Patrick: saints, monasteries, scholarship
   - Source: Schaff's detailed account; Church 101's overview

7. **The Irish Monastic Explosion: Iona and St. Columba (6th–7th Centuries)**
   - Columba's founding of Iona monastery
   - Irish missionary model: monks as agents of Christianization
   - Spread to Scotland and influence northward
   - The Culdees and Celtic Christianity's unique practices
   - Source: Schaff on Irish missions; Church 101 on Scottish conversion

8. **Augustine's Mission to Anglo-Saxon England (A.D. 595–604)**
   - Pope Gregory sends Augustine to Kent
   - Conversion of King Æthelberht
   - Clash: Roman vs. Celtic Christian practices
   - The Synod of Whitby (664): Rome triumphs
   - Source: Schaff's detailed missionary narrative; Church 101 context

9. **The Celtic Church Merges with Rome (7th–11th Centuries)**
   - Gradual conformity to Roman practices
   - Why the independent Celtic Church lost power
   - Subjection of Ireland under English rule
   - Final triumph of Rome: King David I of Scotland (12th century)
   - Source: Schaff on institutional consolidation

### **The Frankish Kingdom & Continental Europe (Lessons 10–13)**

10. **Clovis and the Franks: Alliance of Church & State (A.D. 496)**
    - Why Clovis chose Christianity (political power + spiritual authority)
    - The Frankish kingdom as a Christian power
    - The beginning of Church-State partnership in the West
    - Source: Schaff's account of Frankish conversion; Church 101's framing

11. **Irish Missionaries on the Continent: Columbanus (6th–7th Centuries)**
    - Irish monks as missionaries to Gaul and beyond
    - Columbanus and his followers founding monasteries
    - Spread of Celtic monastic tradition across Europe
    - Eventually absorbed into Benedictine mainstream
    - Source: Schaff's detailed treatment of Irish continental missions

12. **St. Boniface: The Apostle of Germany (A.D. 672–754)**
    - English missionary sent to pagan German tribes
    - Conversion of Frisia, Hesse, Bavaria
    - Organization of dioceses and ecclesiastical structure
    - Martyrdom and legacy
    - Source: Schaff's extensive Boniface chapters; Church 101 overview

13. **Charlemagne, Alcuin, and the Conversion of the Saxons (A.D. 768–814)**
    - Charlemagne's missionary + military campaign against Saxons
    - Forced conversion: baptism or death
    - Educational revival: Alcuin and the palace school
    - The Heliand: Gospel harmony in Saxon vernacular
    - Source: Schaff on Charlemagne's ecclesiastical role; Church 101 on 9th-century learning

### **Northern & Eastern Expansions (Lessons 14–17)**

14. **St. Ansgar and Scandinavian Missions (A.D. 801–865)**
    - "Apostle of the North" to Denmark and Sweden
    - Christianization of Scandinavia: slow, difficult process
    - Norse resistance: heathenism deeply rooted
    - Later conversions in Denmark (10th c.), Sweden, Norway, Iceland
    - Source: Schaff's detailed Scandinavian sections; Church 101 on regional progress

15. **Cyril and Methodius: Apostles of the Slavs (9th Century)**
    - Mission to Moravia and the Balkans
    - Creation of Glagolitic alphabet (Slavic script)
    - Christianization of Moravia, Bohemia, Poland
    - Eastern Slavic Christianity: Byzantine/Orthodox influence
    - Source: Schaff's chapters on Slavic conversion; Church 101 context

16. **Russia's Conversion: Vladimir and Kiev (10th Century)**
    - Vladimir the Great's choice of Orthodox Christianity
    - Connection to Byzantine Empire
    - Birth of Russian Orthodox Church
    - Massive Christianization of Eastern Slavs
    - Source: Schaff on Eastern missions; Church 101's brief treatment

17. **Islam's Rise and Its Relationship to Christianity (7th Century)**
    - Mohammed's life and character
    - Islamic conquests: loss of Christian territories in Asia/Africa
    - The Koran vs. the Bible
    - Mohammedan theology and worship
    - Christian polemics and responses
    - Source: Schaff's substantial Islam section; Church 101 on Gregory and Islam's rise

## **PART III: THE PAPACY & IMPERIAL POWER (Lessons 18–22)**

The institutional drama: popes gaining temporal and spiritual authority, conflicts with emperors, the Great Schism, and the corruption that prompted reform.

18. **Pope Nicolas I and Photios: The Power Struggle (9th Century)**
    - Nicolas I's claim to supreme papal authority
    - The Pseudo-Isidorian Decretals: forged documents backing papal supremacy
    - Patriarch Photios of Constantinople's resistance
    - First major East-West conflict
    - Source: Church 101 on 9th-century struggle; Schaff on papal rise

19. **The Donation of Pepin and the Papal States (8th Century)**
    - Pepin's military alliance with the Papacy
    - Creation of the Papal States as temporal territory
    - Church-State partnership reaches institutional level
    - Source: Schaff on Church-State relations

20. **Charlemagne Crowned Emperor (A.D. 800) and the Holy Roman Empire**
    - Christmas Day 800: Leo III crowns Charlemagne Emperor
    - What this meant: Christian empire, Church legitimizes imperial rule
    - Papal authority seemingly above emperor
    - Later complications: conflict over supremacy
    - Source: Schaff's detailed account; Church 101 on Charlemagne

21. **The Degradation of the Papacy (10th Century Dark Ages)**
    - "Dark Ages": papal corruption, political interference from Rome families
    - Popes elected/deposed by local Rome powers
    - Otto the Great's intervention: emperor controls pope
    - Crisis of credibility and spiritual authority
    - Source: Church 101 on "Dark Ages"; Schaff's documentation of corruption

22. **The Great Schism (1054) and East-West Division**
    - Centuries of tension: theological, liturgical, jurisdictional
    - Photios's 9th-century resistance echoed
    - Patriarch Cerularius vs. papal legate
    - Final break: Greek East from Latin West
    - Consequences for medieval Christianity
    - Source: Church 101 on the Schism; Schaff on its causes

## **PART IV: DOCTRINE, HERESY & CONTROVERSY (Lessons 23–26)**

The theological ferment: major doctrinal disputes that shaped medieval Christianity.

23. **The Filioque Controversy (8th–9th Centuries)**
    - Technical question: does the Holy Spirit proceed from the Father only, or Father AND Son?
    - Why it mattered: theological precision + papal authority
    - Charlemagne's court defended "Filioque"
    - Eastern patriarchs opposed it
    - Contributed to Great Schism
    - Source: Schaff's detailed theology sections; Church 101 overview

24. **Eucharistic Theology and Transubstantiation**
    - Paschasius Radbertus: real transformation of bread/wine
    - Ratramnus: symbolic memorial (moderate view)
    - Berengar's controversy (11th century)
    - Lanfranc and the triumph of Transubstantiation doctrine
    - Source: Schaff's extensive eucharistic controversy chapters

25. **Predestination & Free Will Debates**
    - Gottschalk vs. Hincmar: tension between divine foreknowledge and human choice
    - Competing theories: Augustinianism, Semi-Pelagianism, Pelagianism
    - Medieval settlement: semi-Augustinian compromise
    - Source: Schaff's detailed predestination section

26. **Heretical Movements: Paulicians, Euchites, Manichaeans**
    - Early medieval heresies challenging orthodoxy
    - Dualism: matter vs. spirit
    - Threat from East and West
    - Orthodoxy's response: formal condemnation
    - Preview of later medieval heresies
    - Source: Schaff's heresy chapters; Church 101 context

## **PART V: CHURCH LIFE, WORSHIP & CULTURE (Lessons 27–30)**

How medieval Christians actually lived faith: the sensory, sacramental, intellectual dimensions.

27. **The Mass, Liturgy & Worship in Medieval Christianity**
    - The Latin Mass as central act of medieval piety
    - Sacraments: seven-fold medieval understanding
    - The priest's power to consecrate (transubstantiation)
    - Participation of laity (mostly passive)
    - Source: Schaff on medieval worship; Church 101's various liturgy references

28. **The Veneration of Saints and the Iconoclastic Controversy (8th Century)**
    - Why icons and saint veneration became controversial in the East
    - The Iconoclastic period: destruction of images
    - Restoration: Seventh Ecumenical Council (787) allows icons
    - Western vs. Eastern approaches to sacred images
    - Source: Church 101's dedicated iconoclasm chapter; Schaff's theological treatment

29. **Monasticism at Its Height: Cluny and Spiritual Reform (10th–11th Centuries)**
    - Cluny monastery: response to 10th-century decay
    - Monastic reform movement: return to strict Rule
    - Monasteries as centers of scholarship and spirituality
    - Political influence: monks as papal advisors
    - Source: Church 101 on Cluny; Schaff on monasticism's role

30. **Learning, Literacy & Medieval Scholarship**
    - Monastic and cathedral schools as only educational institutions
    - Charlemagne's educational reforms and Alcuin
    - Preservation of classical texts in monasteries
    - Figures: Bede, Alcuin, John Scotus Erigena, Gerbert
    - Preview of scholasticism (Thomas Aquinas comes later)
    - Source: Schaff's "State of Learning" chapters; Church 101 on intellectual figures

## **DESIGN PHILOSOPHY**

- **Balance sources**: Church 101 provides century-by-century framework; Schaff provides depth, figures, theology
- **Emphasis on conversion narratives**: Schaff's heart is missionary work—this is the arc of medieval Christianity
- **Three pillars structure**: Papacy (institutional power), Monasticism (spiritual power), Scholasticism (intellectual power)
- **Both East and West**: Not just Rome-centric; includes Slavic, Byzantine, Orthodox developments
- **Concrete figures**: Gregory, Patrick, Columba, Boniface, Ansgar, Cyril, Methodius, Nicholas I, Charlemagne, Francis (comes in lesson 30)
